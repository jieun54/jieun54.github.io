I am a Ph.D. candidate at the [University of Michigan School of Information](https://www.si.umich.edu/), working with [Libby Hemphill](https://www.libbyh.com/). As a computational social scientist, I integrate computational methods with social science theory to understand how human behavior and social interaction in online spaces emerge through the interplay of users, social contexts, and technologies.

My doctoral research focuses on (1) how technological interventions, such as platform-specific features and general-purpose technologies, interact with users’ practices to shape participation in online communities and the benefits users derive from that participation, and (2) how these processes vary according to users’ existing status and roles. Methodologically, I use data science techniques to collect and analyze large-scale digital trace data. I generate social scientific insights about how users interact with both people and AI in online spaces and translate those insights into evidence-based design recommendations for platforms and practitioners.
